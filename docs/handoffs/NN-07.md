`tensor/transform.go` adds private shared transform validation/copying and
`normalizeAxes(axes []int64, rank int) (normalized []int, err error)`. This helper
preserves axis order, normalizes with `NormalizeAxis`, and rejects duplicates
after normalization. Empty axes are valid even for rank zero; nonempty axes
require a positive rank. Later multi-axis kernels may reuse it.

`tensor/transform_test.go`, `tensor/testdata/transforms.json`, and its original
`generate_transforms.py` supply offline fixtures and explicit contract tests.
`tensor/testdata/README.md` records provenance; `docs/tensor-design.md` records
the shipped subset. No matrix, activation, layer, training, or serialization
contract changed, and no module dependency was added.

## Ownership and supported cases

Both float32 and int64 are preserved without conversion. Every destination
owns separate storage; reshape, flatten, squeeze, and unsqueeze copy values
even when only metadata differs. Transpose materializes row-major order into
the caller-owned destination. There are no metadata-only aliases or public
strides/views. Inputs, dimensions, axes, and permutations remain unchanged.
Shape planners return copied metadata and allocate no numeric payloads.

Planners check the int64 payload byte bound so their shapes support both types.
They reuse checked dimension/products/sums and host-index validation. Removing
or inserting ones preserves an already checked payload count. Flatten checks
both prefix and suffix products, even when the other is zero. Empty transpose
validates permutations/type/shape/alias rules then skips unused strides.

All tensor, type, exact destination shape, axes/permutation, size, and storage
identity validation precedes writes. Returned errors preserve destinations.
Pointer and shallow-copy aliases are rejected, including identity operations
and empty tensors. Scalars and zero dimensions retain concrete counts and
exact typed order. Owned outputs survive later calls. Concurrent callers may
share immutable inputs/metadata with disjoint outputs; there is no shared
mutable scratch, retained storage, workspace, or training state.

Reshape takes a fully resolved nonnegative concrete target from the destination;
zero dimensions are literal and `-1` is invalid. Flatten accepts split axes in
`[-rank, rank]`, including both endpoints and scalar axis zero. Transpose needs
a full explicit permutation with normalized negative axes. Squeeze removes
exactly listed unit axes; unsqueeze normalizes against the final output rank.
Empty axis lists are identity copies. The runtime resolves omitted-axis and
ONNX-specific shape/default policy before calling the library.
